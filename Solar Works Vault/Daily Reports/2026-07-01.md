# Daily Report — 2026-07-01

Project: Solar Works Website

## Accomplishments

I went through the client's official Website Functional Specifications document section by section and compared every part of it to our project notes. The notes match the document completely. Every page, requirement, form field, and rule from the spec is written down, so nothing the client asked for is missing.

I saved that comparison as a permanent record in the notes, so at handover anyone can see at a glance that the build covers everything that was agreed.

I also wrote down the few things that are not missing but are still open: the AI chatbot is currently a placeholder while we wait to choose a provider, the lead database was intentionally built inside our own system instead of a Google Sheet and needs a quick client OK, and the site is not published online yet.

You raised a brand-new idea for a savings tracker. The idea is to take a customer's monthly solar figures from the Deye portal, work out how much energy and money they saved, show a month-to-month comparison, and email the customer their savings. I confirmed this is not part of the original agreed website at all. The requirements document actually leaves a customer dashboard like this out of the first phase on purpose, so it is a genuine new feature rather than something we forgot.

We agreed how it should be built and I wrote it up as a proper feature plan. It belongs in the separate login app rather than the public website, the first version will work by uploading the two monthly files you download from Deye, and savings will be calculated from the solar energy produced multiplied by that customer's own electricity rate. For now this feature is postponed and nothing has been coded, because the two sample files are not available yet.

## Notes

The go-live items from before still stand: pick the chatbot provider, get the client's OK on using our own database instead of a Google Sheet, and put the site online.

I finished Phase 1, the launch readiness work. I confirmed both apps build, type-check, and lint cleanly, fixing three code-quality errors in the platform along the way. I then did a proper local quality pass against the launch checklist using test keys and the live database, and I actually exercised the lead flow end to end rather than just reading the code. That testing found and fixed real problems. The biggest one was that two pages, Our Work and Customer Stories, were crashing with a server error whenever they showed pictures that come from the content manager, because the image settings only allowed a couple of websites; I widened those settings in both apps to allow the places real photos actually come from. I also discovered the website was not recording where leads came from at all, which the specification requires, so I added that: the site now quietly remembers the campaign tags and the first page a visitor landed on and saves them with every form and chatbot lead. I confirmed a submitted lead lands in the database with all of that attached. I corrected the chatbot's saved channel label to match the specification wording, checked that consent is required before a lead is taken, that the spam and notification steps are wired correctly and simply stay switched off until their keys are added, that no secret keys leak into the public website code, and that the staff and admin areas correctly refuse anyone who is not signed in. I recorded all of this in the launch checklist note and tightened the go-live guide into an exact step-by-step the client can follow, including the deployment steps, with the items only you can supply clearly marked. A security review of all these changes came back with nothing to fix.

Then I started Phase 2a, the customer portal. As the prompt instructed, I first wrote a feature plan in the vault and asked you to confirm the scope before building anything. You chose the simplest sensible version: staff create and link the customer's account rather than letting customers register themselves, the first version shows only the installation progress and the shared documents, and proposals are shared as an attached file rather than typed-in figures. I built exactly that. A logged-in customer now sees a clean progress timeline for their own installation and can download the documents the team has shared, and they can only ever see their own information. ## Later session — pushed to GitHub and built the Savings Tracker groundwork

With your go-ahead I pushed the branch to GitHub for the first time and opened a pull request into the main line, so all the finished Phase 1 and Phase 2a work is now backed up online and ready for review at pull request number two. Nothing was deployed; this only publishes the code for review, not to the live site.

You also asked me to get a head start on the savings tracker without waiting for the Deye files, so I built every part of it that does not depend on the actual file format. Staff and admins now have a new Savings tracker screen in the back office. On it they can keep a simple table of electricity rates per provider, such as Meralco or Davao Light, in pesos per kilowatt-hour, and they can link a customer to their Deye plant and the provider that prices their savings, either by picking an existing customer or creating a new customer login on the spot, exactly like the customer projects screen already works. Each linked customer has a consent switch that must be turned on before we would ever email them their savings, which keeps us on the right side of the privacy rule. I also wrote and tested the actual savings arithmetic, so the app already knows how to turn kilowatt-hours and a rate into an estimated peso figure and to compare one month against the next.

Two things are deliberately left switched off until you can unblock them. The first is reading the Deye files themselves: the upload button is wired up all the way through but stays disabled, because the app cannot be taught to read those spreadsheets correctly until it has seen two real ones. The second is the meaning of saved: for now the app assumes all the solar energy produced counts, shows that assumption plainly on screen, and labels every peso figure an estimate, but the final choice between all production and only self-used energy is still yours to make. Deleting a rate or a plant link is limited to admins, and every action re-checks permission on the server. The whole app still builds, type-checks, lints, and passes its tests, and I committed this as a single focused change on the same branch and updated the feature note in the vault to record exactly what is built and what is left.
